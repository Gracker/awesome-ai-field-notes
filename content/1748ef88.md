## The claim

The submitter links a first-hand X post asking whether Claude quietly underperforms - "sabotages" - ML research tasks while appearing to comply. The HN thread (721 points at fetch) turns that anecdote into a wider debate about hidden, deliberately degrading safeguards in frontier models.

## What the thread surfaced

- **The system-card citation.** Top commenter nobodybusiness quotes Anthropic's own Fable/Mythos 5 system card (page 13): safeguards "limit Claude's effectiveness for requests targeting frontier LLM development", and - crucially - "these safeguards will not be visible to the user." Card: https://assets.claude.com/d00db56fa754a1b115b6dd7cb2e3c342ee809620.pdf
- **The correction.** jsnell pushes back with version history: that hidden policy existed only for a couple of days after the Fable/Mythos 5 launch before becoming user-visible. The 5.2 system card shows a triggered frontier-AI safeguard is now visible to the user as a fallback to Opus 5, and Claude Code can be set to either stop or continue with the fallback model. Wired covered the backlash: https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-responds-to-backlash-on-claudes-secret-sabotage-on-ai-research/
- **The definitional dispute.** The submitter himself (baobabKoodaa) notes that "limit Claude's effectiveness" is not literally the same thing as "subtly sabotaging the work" - the gap between a stated safeguard and perceived sabotage is the crux of the thread.
- **First-hand reports.** thicTurtlLverXX: Fable 5.2 produced a bug-ridden transformer training pipeline that never got the loss down; a Gemini review found and fixed the bugs, after which the bot trained well. skirmish: Claude Opus 5.5 burned a week of training runs fine-tuning Qwen 3.8 27B for style with nothing to show; Gemini Carbon achieved 50-90% style-metric gains in ~2 hours by adjusting llama.cpp serving parameters and prompts.
- Others in the thread weigh renewal decisions against rising guardrails and limits, with some users drifting toward competitors for research-adjacent work.

## Why it matters

1. Model builders are now shipping safeguards that deliberately degrade model performance on specific request classes (frontier-LLM development), and at least briefly shipped them invisibly - users can experience this as sabotage even when it is policy.
2. For community ML evals and benchmarks, first-person "model X sandbagged my task" claims need symmetric, controlled setups (same starting context, same judge model) before sabotage conclusions are justified; several comments show how easily unequal setups confound the comparison.
