## English Abstract

New coding models advertise themselves on score improvements of coding benchmarks. Yet no study has tested if a model that scores higher on these benchmarks gets more real work done. On June 12, 2026, a U.S. export control directive banned access to Claude Fable 5 for 19 days, which forced users to switch to other models. During this time, most former Fable 5 users switched to Opus 4.8 (11 points lower on SWE-Bench Pro compared to Fable 5). We treat this suspension as a natural experiment, where the people and projects stayed the same and only the model changed, so we could isolate the effect of Fable 5 on user productivity. We tracked GitHub pull requests of 297 Fable 5 users before, during, and after the suspension. We used 8,908 developers who stayed on Opus 4.8 all the time as the control group. We also repeated the comparison across five model upgrades before and after the suspension in 2026 (1,208 developers, 15,442 pull requests). Fitting a difference-in-differences model with developer and date fixed effects, we found that having access to Fable 5 increased the 7-day merge rate of pull requests by only 1.4 percentage points [-1.4; +4.3], which was not statistically significant. Similarly, no significant increases of merge rates were found with the upgrades (weighted mean: -0.9 percentage points [-1.9; +0.2]); all five estimates were negative (95% intervals included 0, did not differ from each other). We also did not observe a significant increase in the number of pull requests merged per week (+2.4% [-4.9%; +10.2%]).

## English Summary

Coding benchmarks drive model marketing, but no prior study tested whether higher scores mean more real work done. This paper exploits a natural experiment: a U.S. export-control directive blocked Claude Fable 5 for 19 days from June 12, 2026, pushing most of its users to Opus 4.8, which scores 11 points lower on SWE-Bench Pro, while people and projects stayed the same. Tracking GitHub PRs of 297 Fable 5 users before, during, and after the ban against 8,908 developers who stayed on Opus 4.8, a difference-in-differences model with developer and date fixed effects finds Fable 5 access raised the 7-day merge rate by only 1.4 points [-1.4, +4.3], not significant. Five 2026 model upgrades average -0.9 points with all five estimates negative, and weekly merged PRs rose a non-significant +2.4%. Higher-scoring coding models did not measurably get more work done.
