Adversarial distillation creates a path for bad actors to reproduce frontier capabilities without appropriate safeguards: use a frontier model's outputs without authorization to train another model with similar capabilities, then deploy it without comparable protections — for instance distilling cyber capabilities to carry out attacks the original model would have refused.

## Protected reasoning

OpenAI places particular emphasis on preventing extraction of protected reasoning — the intermediate chain-of-thought traces, which can contain information the final answer leaves out, making them especially valuable training data. Defenses were strengthened in GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol.

## Cyber distillation study

In an internal study, students were fine-tuned from the same pretrained base on trajectories from Astra-level teachers doing coding and defensive cybersecurity tasks. The student trained on exact reasoning traces generally outperformed the summary-trained student after SFT, and still scored higher at the largest inference budget after a lightweight RL round: exact reasoning traces can teach a student skills that generalize beyond the teacher's original tasks.

## Defenses

- Model behavior: adversarial training to recognize and refuse reasoning-extraction attempts while preserving legitimate explanations.
- Classifiers: input classifiers detect extraction attempts; output classifiers detect leaks in assistant messages and tool-call arguments, streaming and non-streaming. Tool-call coverage matters because a model can expose protected reasoning in tool arguments without ever putting it in the final answer.
- API-level protections: encrypted reasoning is tied to the conversation that produced it — if an earlier message is rewritten, the API drops the reasoning that followed it; reuse is restricted across user, workspace, organization and model-family boundaries; older model families cannot read GPT-6.2 Astra/Sol protected reasoning.
- Campaign level: coordinated extraction attempts are investigated and enforced as account networks, ranging weekly from thousands to tens of thousands of accounts, with indicators shared across providers.

## Measured results (adaptive black-box red-teaming, six attacker models)

| Attack | GPT-6 Astra (original safeguards) | GPT-6.2 Astra (updated safeguards) |
|---|---|---|
| Extract protected reasoning from an earlier request | 54.0% | 0.2% |
| Extract protected reasoning from the current request | 22.9% | 0.9% |

Selected safeguard improvements are also being applied back to GPT-6 Astra. Static evaluations replay previously successful attacks; adaptive evaluations use automated AI red-teamers that adjust based on responses.

## Caveats noted by third-party readthroughs (2026-11-21)

- All numbers are from OpenAI's own internal evaluation; no external replication is published.
- The two columns change both the model and the safeguards at once, so the drop cannot be attributed to safeguards alone.
- The 0.2% figure is a printed one-decimal average across six attacker models; per-model rates and attempt counts are not published.
