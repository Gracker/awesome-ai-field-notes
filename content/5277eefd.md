SO Is Back! No, Not Stack Overflow, but Its Spirit

 

One thing people complained about most on Stack Overflow was having their questions closed and marked as duplicates of other questions that were only slightly related. The poster would argue in the comments, trying to make the case that their question was different, only for it to fall on deaf ears.

Then LLMs came along and ate Stack Overflow's lunch. An LLM will answer your question, and with even more vigor if a related question exists online. Everyone was happy, until the LLMs suddenly stopped responding.

Along the way, we decided that LLMs are too dangerous and shouldn't answer every development question. If your prompt sounds, even vaguely, like you are trying to circumvent security, the model drops your request and flags your account. Do it enough times, and your account is closed for good.

Now it's no longer smug developers closing questions. You are left with an unanswered prompt and no one to pin the fault on.

Even when they don't blatantly stop you in your tracks, the answers can feel gated, as if the model knows the answer but deliberately won't tell you.

Now, I was never so much against marking questions as duplicates. In the majority of cases I encountered, the person asking could have benefited from reading the original question. You don't get a full answer, but it serves as a guide that helps you find a solution. With LLMs withholding answers, the fix is often to pay for a more expensive model. So maybe it's not so much Stack Overflow as ExpertSexChange that is back.
