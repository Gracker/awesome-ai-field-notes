## English Abstract / Summary

Cory Doctorow argues the property-rights frame for AI scraping makes workers lose, because privacy is a right not property and cannot be sold like labor. Media groups pushing "unlicensed taking" rhetoric end up putting workers in the position of bargaining subjects for AI-replacement licensing fees; only the Hollywood writers' strike won via sectoral bargaining and union power rather than property-rights framing. Doctorow reframes scraping as a public-interest research tool, citing Ethan Zuckerman's move of the entire platform-power lab from UMass to McGill with $504M Canadian government funding under US political pressure on "unpermissioned research," and notes Wikipedia is being DDoS'd by AI scrapers rather than merely "stolen from."
