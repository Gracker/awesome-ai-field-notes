# AI and Irontown

written on November 09, 2026

For our last movie night we watched [Princess Mononoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Mononoke). I remembered loving that movie when I was younger, but watching it as an adult made me once again realize how Miyazaki has a way of expressing diverging sides without picking one. The film shows people, animals and spirits with legitimate and largely incompatible needs, and each faction is further engaged in infighting.

A central arc of the story is Lady Eboshi’s Irontown, which gives people who were largely rejected by society work, protection and dignity. She gives shelter and work to those who have leprosy and to former sex workers. The mine and the weapon factory provide them with a livelihood, but they also destroy the forest around them. The forest’s inhabitants, in particular the boars, response choose war against the humans to defend themselves. Eboshi is not portrayed as a villain. She does real good ...

## English Summary

Armin Ronacher's Nov 9 essay borrows Irontown from Princess Mononoke as an AI metaphor: Lady Eboshi shelters society's outcasts and gives them work and dignity while destroying the forest and provoking the boar uprising - she is no villain, yet genuinely harms her surroundings. The film's real driver is fear that someone else wins first: nobody needed to kill the forest spirit, yet someone would for an unnecessary prize, with helpers blind to what they would unleash; Ashitaka, who only wants to see clearly, is shot by both sides. His own stance is restrained: he uses AI daily and feels empowered, while conceding the costs are real and largely borne by people who receive none of the benefits.
