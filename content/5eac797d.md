## English Summary

arxiv 2608.02645 challenges the assumption that tool calls are atomic. Existing agent frameworks treat them as binary success/failure, but real systems exhibit timeouts after dispatch, delayed visibility, and partial state updates: a request may have taken effect even when no response came back. The paper shows that pure retry baselines produce more duplicate side-effects under higher failure rates; verify-only (just check, no retry) outperforms retry-only; and a lightweight verify-before-retry wrapper cuts duplicate side-effects while preserving task success. Evaluated in a controlled simulator with injected non-atomic failures across multiple task templates. Engineering implication: error responses are not operation failures recovery decisions must be based on external state (read-before-write diff), with idempotency keys as a safety net.
