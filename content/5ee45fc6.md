## English Abstract (from PDF)

We prove that the exponent of square matrix multiplication is 9/4 over every field. The upper bound was known. For each n ≥ 2, a formal arithmetic circuit multiplying two n × n matrices needs at least n^9/4 / 2 multiplication or division gates. The proof shows that a smaller exponent would compress tensor networks enough to violate a lower bound for testing whether the entries in each of several independent lists are distinct.

Theorem 1.1. Over every field F, the exponent of square matrix multiplication is ω_F = 9/4. ... In fact, for every n ≥ 2 a circuit for two n × n matrices requires at least ⌈n^9/4 / 2⌉ multiplication or division gates, even if linear operations are free. The upper bound gives circuits of size O_{F,ε}(n^{9/4+ε}) for each ε > 0.
