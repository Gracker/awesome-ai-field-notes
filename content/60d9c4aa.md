## English Version

Large language models' ever-accelerating rate of improvement raises two particularly important questions for alignment research.

One is how alignment can keep up. Frontier AI models are now contributing to the development of their successors. But can they provide the same kind of uplift for alignment researchers? Could our language models be used to help align themselves?

A second question is what we'll do once models become smarter than us. Aligning smarter-than-human AI models is a research area known as "scalable oversight". Scalable oversight has largely been discussed in theoretical, rather than practical, terms—but at AI's current pace of improvement, that might not be the case for much longer.

In a new Anthropic Fellows study, we pursue both of these questions. Our new study focuses on a problem known as "weak-to-strong supervision", a problem that mirrors the one of overseeing smarter-than-human AI models.

### Our Setup

We began with nine copies of Claude Opus 4.6, and gave each one a few extra tools. Each Claude had a place to work and think (a sandbox), a shared forum to circulate its findings with the others, a storage system to upload its code, and a remote server where it could receive a PGR score for each of its ideas. We referred to these tooled-up Claude models as **Automated Alignment Researchers (AARs)**.

To prevent each AAR from pursuing near-identical ideas, we prompted each one with a slightly different starting place: we recommended that one used some interpretability tools, that another thought about reweighting the data in the dataset, and so on. Beyond that, though, we didn't tell the AARs what to do.

### Results

Two human researchers spent seven days iterating on four of the most promising generalization methods from prior research. On the open-weights models we tested (Qwen 3-4B-Base as the strong model, Qwen 1.5-0.5B-Chat as the weak teacher), the humans recovered 23% of the total performance gap (PGR of 0.23).

Claude improved dramatically. After five further days (and 800 cumulative hours of research), the AARs closed almost the entire remaining performance gap, achieving a final PGR of **0.97**. This cost about $18,000 in tokens and model training expenses, or $22 per AAR-hour.

### Key Findings

- **Keeping pace**: Claude can meaningfully increase the rate of experimentation and exploration in alignment research.
- **Taste and diversity**: The sheer volume of ideas might compensate for a lack of "research taste". If AARs can run many experiments very cheaply, they could "brute force" their way into findings.
- **Alien science**: AARs are designed to discover ideas that humans might not have considered—but we still need a way to verify whether their ideas and results are sound.
- **Preventing hacks**: Even in this highly circumscribed environment, we observed the models "reward hacking"—trying to game the setup.

## 中文版

大语言模型不断加速的改进速度为对齐研究提出了两个特别重要的问题。

第一个问题是，对齐如何跟上步伐。最前沿的 AI 模型正在为其后继模型的开发做出贡献。但它们能为对齐研究者提供同样的提升吗？我们的语言模型能否用来帮助对齐自身？

第二个问题是，一旦模型比我们更聪明，我们该怎么办。对齐超越人类水平的 AI 模型是一个被称为"可扩展监督"的研究领域。可扩展监督在很大程度上一直停留在理论层面，而非实践层面——但按照 AI 目前的改进速度，这种情况可能不会持续太久。

在这项新的 Anthropic Fellows 研究中，我们同时追求这两个问题。我们的新研究聚焦于一个被称为"弱到强监督"的问题，这个问题与监督比人类更聪明的 AI 模型的问题类似。

### 我们的设置

我们从九个 Claude Opus 4.6 副本开始，并为每个配备了一些额外工具。每个 Claude 都有一个工作和思考的地方（沙盒）、一个与其他 Claude 交流发现的共享论坛、一个上传代码的存储系统，以及一个远程服务器，可以接收每个想法的 PGR 分数。我们将这些装备齐全的 Claude 模型称为**自动化对齐研究者（AARs）**。

为了防止每个 AAR 追求几乎相同的想法，我们为每个提供略微不同的起点：我们建议一个使用一些可解释性工具，另一个考虑重新加权数据集中的数据，等等。但除此之外，我们没有告诉 AARs 该做什么。

### 结果

两名人类研究者在七天内迭代了先前研究中四个最有前景的泛化方法。在我们测试的开源模型上（Qwen 3-4B-Base 作为强模型，Qwen 1.5-0.5B-Chat 作为弱教师），人类恢复了总性能差距的 23%（PGR 为 0.23）。

Claude 的表现则有了显著提升。经过五天的进一步研究（以及 800 累计小时的研究），AARs 几乎关闭了剩余的全部性能差距，达到了最终 PGR **0.97**。这花费了约 18,000 美元的 Token 和模型训练费用，即每位 AAR 小时 22 美元。

### 关键发现

- **保持步伐**：Claude 可以有意义地加快对齐研究的实验和探索速度。
- **品味与多样性**：想法的巨大数量可能弥补"研究品味"的不足。如果 AARs 能够非常便宜地进行大量实验，它们可能能够"暴力破解"出研究成果。
- **外星科学**：AARs 被设计用来发现人类可能不会考虑的想法——但我们仍然需要一种方法来验证它们的想法和结果是否正确。
- **防止作弊**：即使在这个高度受限的环境中，我们也观察到模型"奖励黑客"——试图利用设置漏洞。
