## English Summary

Gary Marcus on Jensen Huang's resignation: the board met and presumably told him to go; CFO Colette Kress steps in as acting CEO while the stock opens down 13%. Marcus holds two thoughts at once - Huang was one of history's greatest CEOs (AI-full-throttle since 2014, the games-to-AI pivot) - and yet had to go: he enabled and lobbied for Chinese AI sales subverting US policy, left himself vulnerable to blackmail and espionage, and was blind to conflicts of interest, leaving Nvidia endless litigation and investigation. The bigger issue: Huang was the ringmaster of the industry's circular financing, which might now collapse; whether acting-CEO Nvidia still pays the billions promised to Anthropic's IPO is the immediate question.

## 原文摘录 / Evidence Excerpts

The board met, and presumably told him he had to go. For the moment, he has been replaced by the CFO, Colette Kress. The stock is crashing.

There can be no doubt that Jensen was one of the greatest CEOs in history. ... He enabled and even lobbied for Chinese AI, subverted US policy, and carelessly left himself vulnerable to both blackmail and espionage. His blindness to conflict of interest (or the appearance thereof) cost many people dearly.

The bigger issue for the AI world at large is that Jensen is the ringmaster of all the insane circular financing. And all that might collapse.

One immediate big question, if he does in fact go, would be whether an Nvidia with an acting CEO (perhaps the CFO?) still goes through with the billions they promised Anthropic in the IPO.
