## English Summary

Simon Willison excerpts the "Approach to compute" section of Anthropic's IPO roadshow video (no official transcript; he made one with MacWhisper). Co-founder Tom Brown: Claude trains and serves across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and AMD GPUs - an early unconventional multi-chip bet. This year, first AMD onboarding took Mythos three days with basically no human intervention, Claude porting or writing almost 100% of the kernels; past chip platforms took dozens of engineers months. Second concept: compute multipliers - total effective compute = physical capacity x discovered multipliers; a 4x multiplier trains the same model with 25 units instead of 100. Multipliers stack into a hard-to-replicate advantage, and Anthropic believes Claude will discover them by itself approaching recursive self-improvement.

## 原文摘录 / Evidence Excerpts

We train and serve Claude now across all four major chip families. That's only possible because early on we made an unconventional bet on a multi-chip strategy.

This year, we started working with AMD GPUs for the first time. In the past, bringing on a new chip platform took dozens of engineers months of work. This time, we had Mythos try it out. It got Claude running on AMD in just three days, with basically no human intervention. Claude ported or wrote almost 100% of the kernels.

We focus on total effective compute, which is the multiplicative product of physical capacity times the compute multipliers that we discover. ... say you need 100 units of compute to train a model of a certain intelligence. A 4x compute multiplier means you can use 25 units of compute to train the exact same model.

We believe that this can accelerate even further as we approach recursive self-improvement. Claude will be able to discover compute multipliers entirely by itself.
