## English Excerpt

"The difference is that an AI agent like Opus 5.5 can spawn dozens of copies of itself to read through sources in multiple languages. If you give these agents an API key, they can also run their own embedding searches on new sources that emerge during their research, as Opus did here during the run that led it to the newly-discovered passage."

"The journal was probably written by Isbrant Cornelisz van Petten, who captained a Dutch East India Company merchant vessel called Wapen van Amsterdam. The Wapen made landfall on the island of Mauritius in April, 1615 [...] Among other things, they 'caught many tortoises, dodos [dodeersen], and some geese and parrots.'"

## English Summary

Historian Benjamin Breen documents the full method chain behind frontier models producing genuinely new historical knowledge: define a specific research question from specialist knowledge, pick a large well-edited corpus (the GLOBALISE VOC archive), run embedding-based semantic search, have frontier models read and rank candidate passages for human review, then iterate. Opus 5.5 spawned parallel copies reading multilingual sources and ran its own embedding searches on newly surfaced corpora, locating a previously unnoticed 1615 Dutch ship log (Wapen van Amsterdam) whose crew 'caught many tortoises, dodos (dodeersen), and some geese and parrots' on Mauritius a new eyewitness record beyond the known dodo source base. Breen stresses the epistemological weirdness: novel results in a format alien to academic writing (alongside GPT-6 Astra breaking a Napoleonic-era cipher in six hours).
