Borretti argues the timeline debate skips a prior question: we do not know what a superintelligence would look like. From the plain definition - AI far above top humans on nearly all cognitive tasks - little follows. Human genius gives a lower bound, not an upper one; solid domain theories exist only in a few places, so whether capacities like superpersuasion exist is an empirical question. He models discovery as a function of intelligence, existing knowledge, and observable data, with unknown coefficients. The sharpest move: whether an ASI can do X usually fails because our theory of X itself is thin - the set of things an ASI can do is a subset of what is possible, and delineating it deductively requires a complete theory of X's domain.
