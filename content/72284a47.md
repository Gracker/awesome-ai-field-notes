Google is rebranding its Spark AI agent [as “Tasks”](https://gemini.google/overview/productivity/) and making it free to use. The [feature, which is powered](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/) by Google’s new Gemini 4 Hydrogen AI model, can do things like create meal plans, manage your calendar, and dig through your emails to find recurring expenses you might not need.

Users in the US will get access to a few other features, including the ability for the AI agent to call businesses to compare product pricing and check inventory, purchase items with Google Pay, and sign into accounts that support Google Password Manager. US users can also assign Gemini tasks by emailing gemini@google.com or texting it.

Video: Google

Google first introduced [its Spark AI agent in May](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/941388/gemini-spark-ai-agent-trip-planning), but it was only available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The launch of tasks in Gemini brings Spark’s capabilities to all users in the US, Canada, Japan, Korea, Australia, and India. You can find tasks by opening Gemini and changing the toggle on the homepage from “Chat” to “Tasks.”

Video: Google

Tasks in Gemini is just one of the many agentic AI features that have emerged over the past year, [including Claude Duet,](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032518/claude-duet-ai-agent-hands-on) [OpenAI’s Dots](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002033/openai-dots-launch-muse-competitor), and [Meta’s Muse](https://www.theverge.com/column/999999/optimizer-meta-muse-ai-cute). Google says Gemini will perform tasks in a “hardware-isolated virtual machine that’s dedicated to your account and never shared with other users.” You can also provide instructions for Gemini while it completes tasks. When performing more sensitive tasks, like purchasing an item, it will stop working and ask for your permission before following through.
