## English Summary

Cory Doctorow argues that killing Section 230 is exactly what big tech wants. Banning scraping is terrible policy: search engines are built on scraping, and a licensing regime for transient copies is one only Google could afford; understanding how algorithms peddle disinformation, how Amazon price-gouges from surveillance data, or how Uber lowballs desperate drivers depends on scraping, and protecting creative livelihoods calls for labor organizing, not more copyright. CDA 230 makes users responsible for their speech and platforms non-liable for carrying it; large platforms with lawyer armies and AI moderation can survive litigation - what they fear is users mass-migrating to small independent services. His 30-year-old mailing-list organization burned much of its assets on one withdrawn lawsuit: without limited liability for intermediaries, there is no independent web.

## Without CDA 230, Facebook will own the internet forever (原文摘录)

Take scraping. Most creative workers are rightly furious at the palpable glee of tech bosses, who want to take everything we've made in order to train models whose express purpose is to destroy our livelihoods and transfer our wages to AI companies and the media companies they sell to.

This is an entirely correct thing to be angry about. But many of my peers and comrades think the answer to this is to ban scraping, which is a terrible idea.

Scraping is how we make search engines. If you want Google to be the last search engine we ever get then by all means, change the rules so you need licenses to make transient copies of the web. There's only one company with deep-enough pockets to secure permission from every web site operator: Google, the thrice-convicted monopolist that dominates the web so thoroughly that no one could afford to turn them down.

Scraping is how we glue down the web's shifting sands. If you want to understand why the web says something today, it helps to know what it said yesterday. If we want to know how Zuck and Musk's algorithms are peddling disinformation and hatred, we need to be able to scrape their feeds. If we want to know how Amazon uses surveillance data to price-gouge shoppers, we need to scrape its prices.

(If you want to protect creative livelihoods from AI bosses, the answer lies in labor organizing, not more copyright.)
