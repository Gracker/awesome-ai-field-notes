## Key claims (English)

CLASHarness generates controllable, verifiable multi-agent conflicts in computer use via hierarchical planning plus an eight-step verified micro-pipeline, yielding 600 multi-round tasks. Evaluating 15 frontier models exposes three bottlenecks: weaker models follow misleading sub-agent output, fixing state is much harder than spotting conflicts, and even frontier models fail over 58% of closing actions such as releasing shared locks.

arXiv abstract page (submitted 13 Oct 2026)

Authors: Yuxiang Lai, Jike Zhong, Chengzhi Liu, Ming Li, Haonan Wang, Xiaofeng Yang, Liang Zhao, Youbao Tang, Ruixi Fan
Comments: 

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems to automate complex computer-use workflows, yet existing benchmarks mostly assume consistent, cooperative environments. In contrast, real-world collaboration rarely runs on consistent information: peer or sub-agents frequently report outdated states, issue contradictory instructions, or compete over shared permissions and files, forcing agents to check others&#39; outputs and verify ground truth in the environment. Evaluating such conflict resolution remains challenging: manual authoring cannot scale across complex systems, while unconstrained LLM generation produces narrative drift, answer leakage, and unsatisfiable rubrics. We introduce CLASHarness (Conflict-Loaded Agent Scenario Harness), a controllable framework for generating verifiable multi-agent conflicts in computer use, with a benchmark of 600 multi-round tasks. By combining hierarchical scenario planning with an eight-step verified micro-pipeline, CLASHarness pre-commits decisive visual facts before rendering and compiles hybrid code and semantic graders directly from executed reference trajectories, guaranteeing every task is solvable by construction. Evaluating 15 frontier models on CLASHarness reveals three core bottlenecks. First, when sub-agent outputs conflict with the environment, weaker models blindly follow misleading information, whereas stronger models inspect visual evidence to verify ground truth. Second, resolving conflicts is much harder than finding them: while agents can spot inconsistencies, actively fixing system state drops task success significantly. Third, coordination protocols remain a universal bottleneck: even frontier models fail over 58% of closing actions, often forgetting to release shared locks or hand off tasks. CLASHarness provides a scalable, verifiable foundation for building reliable multi-agent systems.
