## 4.5 LLM Tradeoffs (原文摘录)

Large Language Models enable small teams to produce software and documentation of staggering scope and complexity in a remarkably short time. Five years ago, writing a distributed SQL database of this scope might have taken hundreds of person-years. With LLM assistance, a handful of engineers at Orkes were able to produce a highly sophisticated database system.

However, our work on ParticleDB (and other databases) suggests there are serious tradeoffs to relying on LLMs. Quality suffered: ParticleDB was a fractal of correctness errors. LLM tooling repeatedly claimed that bugs had been fixed when, in fact, they had not. ParticleDB also seemed more difficult to understand than typical databases. As with most Jepsen analyses, Orkes engineers offered detailed explanations of the causes and resolutions of the bugs we found in Conductor. However, Orkes seemed unable to explain the causes or resolutions of nearly every issue we found in ParticleDB. New builds simply materialized, sans changelogs, each hopefully containing a slew of improvements. Why did ParticleDB sometimes turn strings into integers? Why did it stop in 1.1.18.11.rc-1? Perhaps no one knows.

More broadly, the use of LLMs erodes trust in documentation, in software, and in the organizations which produce them. Perhaps engineers will begin to treat any new software project with suspicion - the signals we used to rely on as markers of a mature system are now easier to counterfeit.
