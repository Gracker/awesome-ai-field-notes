## English Summary

Doug Brown fed a five-year-old BIOS bug on a 2008 eMachines EL1200 board (8GB RAM blocks setup entry; a single 4GB stick hangs the same way) to Claude Opus 5 and had a diagnosis and patch within half an hour; the BIOS dump came from NVIDIA MCP61 + Phoenix/Award, and the flashed board enters setup normally. He contrasts a similar fix seven years ago — a one-line GRUB ACPI hack for Windows bluescreens with 16GB on a nominally 8GB board — which cost hours of staring at assembly. The boundary is explicit: you still need the old hardware that can flash socketed ROMs and the judgment to verify; the agent is the new path through the parts humans can no longer stare at.
