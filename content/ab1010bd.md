# Software engineers don't get to be Luddites about AI

## 要点 (中文)

Sean Goedecke 10 月 12 日文章：反 AI 工程师喜欢自比卢德派纺织工，但更准的历史对照是造机器后被机器自动化掉的机械匠——1830 年起织机取代手工织工，1852 年机械工程师公会又因改良机器让未受训廉价劳力上岗而罢工四个月，工资烧光复工，《泰晤士报》当时就点破『你们造机器让人失业，现在轮到自己』。文章主张软件工程从第一天起就站在自动化一边，反 AI 的职业立场在自身历史面前站不住。

## Key claims (English)

Goedecke counters AI-skeptical engineers: the honest historical analogy is not Luddite weavers but the machine-makers automated by their own improvements, including the 1852 engineers' strike — a profession built on automation doesn't get to opt out.

# Software engineers don't get to be Luddites about AI
> 原文链接: https://seangoedecke.com/software-engineers-dont-get-to-be-luddites-about-ai/

---

Many software engineers are [righteously](https://blog.glyph.im/2026/10/programming-isnt-special.html) [angry](https://deadsimpletech.com/blog/llms-arent-inevitable) about AI. It’s understandable to be [upset](https://www.seangoedecke.com/will-my-job-still-exist/) that the skills you built your professional identity around are getting automated away. But software engineers are the last people in the world who should be complaining about this. If you live by the sword, you will perish by the sword[1](#fn-1).

### Software engineering has always automated industries[](#software-engineering-has-always-automated-industries)

From the very beginning, software engineering has been about automating other people’s work. Every single commercial software program[2](#fn-2) is valuable because it takes over some task you would have to pay a human to do. The engineer who built VisiCalc, one of the first pieces of personal commercial software, [agonized](https://www.northcountrypublicradio.org/news/npr/389027988/) over the potential of the spreadsheet to put bookkeepers out of work. Here is a brief list of industries software engineers have automated away[3](#fn-3):

-   Video rental stores
-   Business directories and encyclopaedias
-   Typesetting companies
-   Travel agencies
-   Record stores
-   Typing pools
-   Data-entry
-   Trading-floor runners

Now that it’s our turn, should we bluster and complain that software engineering is a priceless art that should not be automated? Come off it. No crying in the casino.

### The Luddites and the mechanics[](#the-luddites-and-the-mechanics)

Many software engineers have [adopted](https://tante.cc/) the cause of the Luddites. I’ve written about the Luddites [a few times](https://www.seangoedecke.com/tags/luddites/), but in short: they were a group of professional textile workers who found themselves displaced by the advent of textile automation like power looms, knitting frames, and shearing machines. To software engineers worried about being displaced by automation, the Luddites seem like heroes[4](#fn-4). But software engineers aren’t like the textile workers in this scenario! We are like the smiths and mechanics who built the power looms in the first place.

Ironically, those mechanics were themselves [automated away](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DBD09898F9B0971810B16810C22F537B/S0020859000005782a.pdf/origins_of_the_1852_lockout_in_the_british_engineering_industry_reconsidered.pdf) fifty years after the Luddite uprising, in almost an exact parallel. Improvements to the manufacturing process meant that unskilled boys and labourers — so-called “illegal men” — could now work the machines instead of well-paid mechanics. The ensuing labor dispute was less explosive than the Luddite one: there was no violence, and the striking engineers returned to work four months later when the money ran out.

Part of the reason the mechanics’ rebellion fizzled is that they were justifiably seen as hypocrites[5](#fn-5). As the _Times_ wrote:

> The cry of these intelligent engineers is merely the cry of the handloom weavers — a cry against inevitable and irresistible laws.

### Automation and progress[](#automation-and-progress)

Software engineering is the process of automating tedious tasks. It is the process of replacing human labor with the faster, cheaper labor of the machine. In other words, it is the process of putting people out of work.

It’s easy to forget this when you’re sitting at your computer puzzling over some CSS or a runtime error. From the inside, software engineering feels like the process of elegantly expressing problems in their most natural form, or of solving a magnificently complex puzzle. But that’s just what it’s _like_ to do software engineering. It’s not what software engineering _is_.

The optimistic view of software engineering is that automation is progress. Automation has enabled widespread access to modern medicine, slashed child mortality across the world, massively increased the amount of food production, made space exploration possible, and so on. When industries are killed, [new ones appear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox). The old world is being destroyed, but the new world will be much, much better.

This view of technology is pretty unfashionable today. Some combination of climate change, the 2008 market crash, the negative impacts of social media, and the tech industry leaders’ turn to right-wing politics has made it popular to view technology as a largely negative force. Fair enough! I don’t know which side of this debate is right[6](#fn-6). But I know which side software engineers have always been fighting for, no matter what beliefs they profess: the side of technology and automation. Every commit to an open-source project is another component in the great machine of technological progress.

### Shutting your eyes[](#shutting-your-eyes)

I understand the impulse for software engineers to ignore the long-term effects of their industry and focus on the puzzles. If you think technological progress is bad, but don’t want to quit your job, that’s been the only option. You can’t solve every problem in the world.

But to decide that _this_ is the moment to come out against technological progress, at the moment when the wave of automation crests over our industry… it’s undignified[7](#fn-7). Software engineers have participated in the destruction (or transformation) of several kinds of job over their career. If you think that’s wrong, it’s only fair that you should also destroy (or transform) your own.
