## English Abstract

Coding agents increasingly implement rules written by domain experts, and bugs in such code are silent: it runs and returns a plausible answer. We introduce TEA-Bench-BR, a benchmark built on the Brazilian pathway for early autism (TEA) screening: three fictitious instruments with the structure of real ones, a multi-professional referral flow, and export to FHIR R4 with the national RNDS profiles, all specified in Portuguese. A generator samples each task's parameters (144 tasks, 6,798 hidden cases), and the hidden cases kill all 1,955 non-equivalent mutants of the reference. With Gemma 4 as the agent, the 12B solves 38.0% of single-instrument tasks and none at the FHIR level; the 31B solves 77.8%, 85.2% and 78.7% at levels 1 to 3 and passes over 99% of the hidden cases, its remaining failures mostly in input validation rather than clinical decisions. With the same tasks in English, the 31B solves 57.4% at level 3, as its code validates an identifier less often; the 12B's, more often. The 12B misses 10.10% of the cases that call for referral; the 31B, 0.02%. A contamination probe on a modified PHQ-9 finds no error that follows the original rules.

## English Summary

TEA-Bench-BR tests whether coding agents can turn a branching clinical specification into correct code, built on the Brazilian early autism (TEA) screening pathway: three fictitious screening instruments, a multi-professional referral flow, and FHIR R4 export with national RNDS profiles, all specified in Portuguese. A generator samples 144 tasks with 6,798 hidden cases that kill all 1,955 non-equivalent mutants of the reference implementation. With Gemma 4 agents, the 12B model solves 38.0% of single-instrument tasks and none at the FHIR level; the 31B solves 77.8-85.2% across three levels and passes over 99% of hidden cases, failing mostly at input validation. Switching the same tasks to English drops the 31B to 57.4% at level 3, exposing sensitivity to specification language; the 31B misses 0.02% of referral-required cases versus 10.10% for the 12B.
