## Source Excerpts (opencli web read, 2026-11-25)

I feel like my previous posts (and post titles), and podcasts, and talks, have failed to do full justice to the magnitude of what’s now happening, so I’m trying again.

Last night, we had friends and colleagues over for dinner. We were still trying to process OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s [proofs of VP ≠ VNP](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10362)—hot on the heels, obviously, of the Alpöge/Anthropic [proof of the Riemann Hypothesis](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10344), which already feels like last week’s news. But then before dessert was served, we saw that OpenAI claims to prove that [ω, the exponent of matrix multiplication, is exactly 9/4](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf)—a **lower bound (!!)**, matching the upper bound that OpenAI proved in the great Mathocalypse drop of October 6.

There are two separate shocks here. The first shock is that we now, finally, know the right answer to this central mystery of algorithms going back to the late 1960s, when [Volker Strassen first showed that ω<3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strassen_algorithm) (he got ω≤log27) … and that the true answer is actually a _rational_ number (!). But the even bigger shock is that the AI was able to _prove_ any lower bound of the form ω>2. I never expected this challenge to be all that much easier than P ≠ NP.

Over the decades, I often heard colleagues express extreme confidence that ω=2, and I expressed skepticism whenever this came up—not because I had any strong argument for ω>2, but just because I saw that there was no strong argument for ω=2 either. So, I’m glad to have been vindicated about _that_!

But wait, there was more. During dessert, we saw [another OpenAI release](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/paper.pdf) proving that the true number of linear gates needed to compute the Fourier Transform is N (log N)1/2+o(1). The upper bound here improves, not only over the O(N log N) that stood since the 1960s (i.e., the Fast Fourier Transform or FFT), but also over the O(N log0.9999999999999 N) (give or take some 9’s) that OpenAI published back in the October 6 Mathocalypse. Again, though, the real shock here is that there’s also a **matching lower bound (!!)**. All these questions from the ancient days are now just getting … straight-up _answered_, rather than slowly illuminated with a drip-drip-drip of clues.

To give an idea of how much things have changed, the other day Yupan Liu put a [preprint on the arXiv proving that QIP(2) = PSPACE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31236)—that is, quantum interactive protocols with only two messages have the full power of those with three, or any polynomial number. As recently as 2025, this would’ve considered been one of the top complexity results of the year, and the fact that Yupan had used AI to help achieve it would’ve been strange and wonderful. These days, I had to remind myself it had even happened, and the use of AI was so standard that it didn’t even raise an eyebrow.

It’s now obvious that we’ll be getting, not one-off solutions to this or that complexity question, but a whole new continent of mathematical truth, one that we’d previously only glimpsed through fog. Picture van Leeuwenhoek first training a microscope on pondwater, or Galileo first training his telescope on the stars. That might give you _some_ idea of how our resolution into the complexity-theoretic universe, and the whole universe of mathematical truth beyond it, has now improved.

I now feel in my bones, for the first time ever, that a proof of P ≠ NP is not far.

For all I care, despise the AI companies for bulldozing our garden. Or love them for opening a new vista, or anything in between. Just please don’t _deny_ what’s happening. Now that the companies have repeatedly shown that they can back up their claims with Lean certificates, don’t be a raging doofus who seizes on some triviality (e.g., a minor discrepancy between the Lean and informal proofs, or unused sections of the Lean proof) to pretend that dozens of great open problems of math and theoretical computer science are not, in fact, getting solved. Because they _are_ getting solved. It might take us years to _understand_ the solutions, but my working assumption by this point is that any proof that comes directly from OpenAI or Anthropic is correct barring information to the contrary.

Along the way to so many lower bounds that we wondered whether ever we’d live to see, there will also, of course, be further shocking _upper_ bounds: that is, ultimate barriers to lower bounds, also known as “algorithms.” And yes, some of those algorithms will break currently deployed cryptosystems.

Because people have been hounding me about it: yes, I’m virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells in that area. **However,** until I either see details or hear directly from someone at a lab, I feel much, much less confident about the specific _nature_ of those bombshells. I do not wish to be responsible for spreading unverified rumors. And I worry that exact statements of what was done may have been mangled in a game of Telephone.

Right now—this past week—is very much like I would’ve always imagined the beginning of an AI singularity to look and feel like. I just hope we’ll get lucky, and it will “merely” be a singularity for our little corner of the world that involves hard math problems.
