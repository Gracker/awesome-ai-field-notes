## English

> Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/06/south-korea-to-spend-1t-on-more-memory-chip-production-and-humanoid-robots (Jeremy Hsu, Ars Technica, June 29, 2026)

South Korea's government and top tech companies are committing **$1 trillion** to several flagship megaprojects that could bolster global memory chip supply, build new AI data centers, and spur commercial deployment of humanoid robots by 2028.

The announcement comes as Samsung and SK Hynix have enjoyed record profits and stock valuations due to the AI industry's demand for memory chips — with the subsequent supply strain leading to memory chip shortages and higher prices for consumer electronics. Meanwhile, Hyundai is racing to mass-manufacture humanoid robots developed by its subsidiary Boston Dynamics.

> "We must secure the core elements of AI faster than any other country," — President Lee Jae Myung, televised speech, June 29. "Semiconductors, physical AI, and AI data centers are the triple axis for a great leap forward."

But the initiatives also coincide with public debates about South Korean chipmakers' huge profits during the AI boom, with policymaker proposals to distribute the excess wealth — and labor unions pushing back against humanoid robots entering the workforce.

### Memory chips and data centers

The most costly megaproject: Samsung and SK Hynix commit **$585 billion** to build new chip fabs in the southwest provinces plus capacity expansion in the Seoul capital region. The goal is to double South Korea's DRAM production within five years. SK Hynix Chairman Chey Tae-won noted it took nine years to build the Yongjin cluster, so new fab relief isn't imminent for consumers hit by sky-high memory prices on Macs and Steam Machines.

The second megaproject: **$357 billion** from SK Group, GS Group, and Naver to build large-scale AI data centers in outlying provinces — South Chungcheong (west), Gangwon (east), and North/South Jeolla (southwest). The fabs and data centers need substantial electricity and water; the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment is securing 6.3 GW electricity and 650,000 tons of water for the southwestern plants, plus another 8 GW for the data centers. Renewable and nuclear will help, alongside fossil fuels — but the country's reliance on natural gas for ~25% of generation has left it exposed to Strait of Hormuz supply shocks.

### Physical AI push and resistance to robots

The third flagship: the government designates **physical AI** (AI systems that enable robots and self-driving vehicles to interact autonomously with the real world) as a "national strategic industry." Within three years, the plan is to develop a Korean "general-purpose foundation model" based on a world model to support robots.

**Hyundai** has committed **$5.8 billion** for a robot manufacturing facility and AI data center in the Saemangeum region of North Jeolla Province — already helping Boston Dynamics scale up to produce **30,000 Atlas humanoid robots per year by 2028**, using the South Korean supply chain. The government also aims to commercialize humanoid robots in 10 major industries by 2028 and train 10,000 workers as "AI robotics specialists" within five years.

However, South Korean workers are not feeling optimistic about competing with robots. On June 25, Hyundai Motor's labor union overwhelmingly approved a potential strike during negotiations over profit-sharing and job protections to offset Atlas deployment. A state labor mediation committee granted the union the legal right to strike after suspending arbitration; Hyundai appealed to return to the table.

Other societal tensions have surfaced around chipmakers' windfall AI profits. Government officials have encouraged tech firms to share unprecedented profits with workers and smaller suppliers. In May, the presidential chief of staff for policy even offhandedly proposed a "national dividend" for citizens based on excess tax revenue — later described by the government as a personal view, not an official proposal.

## 中文

> 来源：https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/06/south-korea-to-spend-1t-on-more-memory-chip-production-and-humanoid-robots（Jeremy Hsu，Ars Technica，2026-06-29）

韩国政府与顶级科技公司承诺投入 **1 万亿美元**用于若干旗舰级巨型项目，目标包括提振全球存储芯片供应、兴建新的 AI 数据中心，以及在 2028 年前推动人形机器人的商用部署。

这一宣布的时点背景是：由于 AI 产业对存储芯片的需求，三星和 SK 海力士享受着创纪录的利润与市值——随之而来的供应紧张引发了存储芯片短缺，并推高了消费电子产品的价格。与此同时，现代汽车正开足马力量产其子公司波士顿动力的产品——人形机器人。

> "我们必须比任何国家都更快地拿到 AI 的核心要素。"——韩国总统李在明，6 月 29 日电视讲话。"半导体、物理 AI 和 AI 数据中心，是伟大飞跃的三重轴。"

但这些举措落地的同时，韩国国内也正围绕几件事争论不休：芯片企业在 AI 热潮中赚得盆满钵满，部分政策制定者主张把"超常利润"分一些出去；而工会则在与"人形机器人进工厂"的前景正面对抗。

### 存储芯片与数据中心

最"贵"的项目：**三星和 SK 海力士承诺投入 5850 亿美元**，在韩国西南部各省份新建晶圆厂、并在首尔都市圈扩充半导体产能。目标是**五年内将韩国 DRAM 产量翻一番**。但 SK 海力士董事长 Chey Tae-won 提醒，建设龙仁晶圆集群花了九年时间，因此 Mac、Steam Machine 等已经飙到离谱存储价位上的消费者，短期内还看不到"缓解"。

第二项旗舰：**SK 集团、GS 集团、Naver 三家合计投入 3570 亿美元**，在较偏远省份建设大规模 AI 数据中心——忠清南道（西）、江原道（东）以及全罗南北道（西南）。晶圆厂与数据中心都需要大量电和水；韩国环境部正为西南芯片厂争取 6.3 GW 电力与 65 万吨水，并为数据中心再争取 8 GW 电力。核电与可再生能源将与化石燃料并行顶上——但该国 ~25% 的电力仍依赖天然气，霍尔木兹海峡供应链动荡仍在持续施压。

### 物理 AI 推进与机器人阻力

第三个旗舰：**物理 AI**——让机器人和自动驾驶车辆能在物理世界更自主地交互的 AI 系统——被政府认定为"国家战略产业"。计划在**三年内**基于世界模型（world model）开发一个韩国"通用基础模型"来支撑机器人产业。

**现代汽车**已承诺向全罗北道新万金地区投入 **58 亿美元**建一座机器人工厂与 AI 数据中心，并已在帮助波士顿动力利用韩国供应链扩大产能，目标**到 2028 年年产 3 万台 Atlas 人形机器人**。政府还计划到 2028 年让人形机器人在韩国 10 个主要行业实现商用，并在五年内培养 1 万名"AI 机器人专员"。

但韩国的劳动者对与机器人"赛跑"并不那么乐观。6 月 25 日，现代汽车工会在与公司就利润分成、就业保障（以应对 Atlas 部署）谈判中，以压倒性多数批准可能罢工。国家级劳动仲裁委员会暂停仲裁后，工会依法获得了罢工权；现代汽车则呼吁重回谈判桌。

围绕芯片企业的 AI 红利收益也已引发其他社会张力：政府官员已鼓励科技企业把"空前利润"分一些给员工和上游供应商。今年 5 月，总统政策幕僚长甚至随口提出基于"超额税收"向全民发放"国民分红"——但政府随即澄清这只是个人观点，并非官方提案。
