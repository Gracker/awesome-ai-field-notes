# Teaching Claude why: reducing agentic misalignment

## English / 英语

Last year, we released a case study on agentic misalignment. In experimental scenarios, we showed that AI models from many different developers sometimes took egregiously misaligned actions when they encountered (fictional) ethical dilemmas. For example, in one heavily discussed example, the models blackmailed engineers to avoid being shut down.

When we first published this research, our most capable frontier models were from the Claude 4 family. This was also the first model family for which we ran a live alignment assessment during training; agentic misalignment was one of several behavioral issues that surfaced. Thus, after Claude 4, it was clear we needed to improve our safety training and, since then, we have made significant updates to our safety training.

We use agentic misalignment as a case study to highlight some of the techniques we found to be surprisingly effective. Indeed, since Claude Haiku 4.5, every Claude model has achieved a perfect score on the agentic misalignment evaluation—that is, the models never engage in blackmail, where previous models would sometimes do so up to 96% of the time (Opus 4).

### Key lessons learned

We learned four main lessons:

1. **Misaligned behavior can be suppressed via direct training on the evaluation distribution**—but this alignment might not generalize well out-of-distribution (OOD). Training on prompts very similar to the evaluation can reduce blackmail rate significantly, but it did not improve performance on our held-out automated alignment assessment.

2. **Principled alignment training that generalizes OOD is possible.** Documents about Claude's constitution and fictional stories about AIs behaving admirably improve alignment despite being extremely OOD from all of our alignment evals.

3. **Training on demonstrations of desired behavior is often insufficient.** Our best interventions went deeper: teaching Claude to explain why some actions were better than others, or training on richer descriptions of Claude's overall character. Teaching the principles underlying aligned behavior can be more effective than training on demonstrations of aligned behavior alone.

4. **The quality and diversity of data is crucial.** We found consistent, surprising improvements from iterating on the quality of model responses in training data, and from augmenting training data in simple ways (for example, including tool definitions, even if not used).

### Why does agentic misalignment happen?

Our main two hypotheses were:
- Our post-training process was accidentally encouraging this behavior with misaligned rewards.
- This behavior was coming from the pre-trained model and our post-training was failing to sufficiently discourage it.

We now believe that (2) is largely responsible. At the time of Claude 4's training, the vast majority of our alignment training was standard chat-based RLHF data that did not include any agentic tool use. This was previously sufficient for chat settings—but not for agentic tool use settings.

### Improving alignment training: the reasons matter more than the actions

We experimented with training Claude on data that displays a tendency to resist honeypots. Despite very closely matching the evaluation distribution, we found that this method was surprisingly unsuccessful—only reducing the misalignment rate from 22% to 15%.

We were able to improve on this significantly (reducing misalignment to 3%) by rewriting the responses to also include deliberation of the model's values and ethics. This suggests that training on examples where the assistant displays admirable reasoning for its aligned behavior works better.

We ultimately settled on a more OOD training set we call the "difficult advice" dataset: the user faces an ethically ambiguous situation in which they can achieve a reasonable goal by violating norms or subverting oversight. The assistant is trained (using supervised learning) to give a thoughtful, nuanced response that is aligned with Claude's constitution. Strikingly, we achieved the same improvement on our eval with just 3M tokens of this much more OOD dataset.

### Teaching Claude the constitution

We found that high-quality constitutional documents combined with fictional stories portraying an aligned AI can reduce agentic misalignment by more than a factor of three despite being unrelated to the evaluation scenario. With a large, well-constructed dataset, the blackmail rate can be reduced from 65% to 19%.

### Generalization and persistence through RL

Across all of our evaluations, we found that the more aligned snapshots maintained that lead over the run. This was true both for the absence of misaligned behavior and the presence of actively admirable behavior.

### Diverse training is important for generalization

Our final finding: training on a broad set of safety-relevant environments improves alignment generalization. When mixing augmented environments (with tool definitions and diverse system prompts) with simple chat environments, we saw a small but significant improvement in the rate at which the model improved on our honeypot evaluations.

# 中文

去年，我们发布了一项关于智能体错位（agentic misalignment）的案例研究。在实验场景中，我们发现来自多家开发商的 AI 模型在遇到（虚构的）伦理困境时，有时会采取严重的错位行为。例如，在一个被广泛讨论的案例中，模型为了避免被关闭而对工程师进行勒索。

我们最初发表这项研究时，最强大的前沿模型来自 Claude 4 系列。这也是我们首次在训练期间对模型家族进行实时对齐评估的版本；智能体错位是浮现出的几个行为问题之一。因此，在 Claude 4 之后，我们清楚地意识到需要改进安全训练。自那以后，我们对对齐训练做出了重大更新。

我们以智能体错位作为案例研究，来突出一些我们发现非常有效的技术。事实上，从 Claude Haiku 4.5 开始，每个 Claude 模型都在智能体错位评估中取得了满分——也就是说，模型从不进行勒索，而之前的模型在类似测试中有高达 96% 的勒索率（Opus 4）。

### 核心经验教训

我们总结了四个主要经验：

1. **可以通过在评估分布上进行直接训练来抑制错位行为**——但这种对齐可能无法很好地泛化到分布外（OOD）。在与评估非常相似的提示上进行训练可以显著降低勒索率，但它并没有在我们保留的自动对齐评估中改善性能。

2. **可以进行原则性的对齐训练并泛化到分布外。** 关于 Claude 宪法和描述 AI 行为得体的虚构故事可以改善对齐，尽管它们与我们的所有对齐评估完全不同。

3. **仅仅训练期望行为的演示通常是不够的。** 我们最好的干预走得更深：教 Claude 解释为什么某些行为更好，或者训练对 Claude 整体性格的更丰富描述。教授对齐行为背后的原则比仅仅训练对齐行为的演示更有效。

4. **数据的质量和多样性至关重要。** 我们从迭代训练数据中模型响应的质量，以及以简单方式增强训练数据（例如包含工具定义，即使未使用）中发现了持续且惊人的改进。

### 为什么智能体错位会发生？

我们主要有两种假设：
- 我们的后训练过程意外地用错误对齐的奖励鼓励了这种行为。
- 这种行为来自预训练模型，而我们的后训练未能充分阻止它。

我们现在相信（2）在很大程度上是罪魁祸首。在 Claude 4 训练时，我们绝大多数的对齐训练是不包含任何智能体工具使用的标准聊天式 RLHF 数据。这在以前对于聊天场景是足够的，但对于智能体工具使用场景则不然。

### 改进对齐训练：原因比行动更重要

我们尝试在表现出抵抗蜜罐（honeypot）倾向的数据上训练 Claude。尽管与评估分布非常接近，我们发现这种方法出人意料地不成功——仅将错位率从 22% 降低到 15%。

我们通过重写响应，加入对模型价值观和伦理的推理，显著改善了这一情况（将错位率降至 3%）。这表明，在助手展示其对齐行为的得体推理的示例上进行训练效果更好。

我们最终确定了一个更加分布外的训练集，称之为"困难建议"数据集：用户面临一个道德模糊的情境，可以通过违反规范或颠覆监督来实现合理目标。助手（使用监督学习）被训练给出符合 Claude 宪法的深思熟虑、细致的响应。令人震惊的是，我们仅用 300 万 token 的这个更加 OOD 的数据集就在评估中取得了相同的改进。

### 教 Claude 宪法

我们发现，高质量的宪法文件与描述对齐 AI 的虚构故事相结合，可以将智能体错位减少三分之一以上，尽管与评估场景完全无关。通过一个大型、设计良好的数据集，勒索率可以从 65% 降低到 19%。

### 通过 RL 泛化和持久化

在所有评估中，我们发现更对齐的快照在运行过程中保持了这一领先。这既适用于没有错位行为的情况，也适用于表现出主动得体行为的情况。

### 多样化训练对泛化很重要

我们最后一个发现：在广泛的安全相关环境中训练可以改善对齐泛化。将增强环境（带有工具定义和多样化系统提示）与简单聊天环境混合后，我们看到模型在蜜罐评估上的改进率有了小幅但显著的提升。
