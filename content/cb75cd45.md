## 原文摘录

I spent a lot of time thinking about differential history. What it means to change the world isn't to have a pile of stuff that people say is yours or a pile of accomplishments that people say are yours. That's just credit assignment, which is notoriously hard even without all the adversarial noise. No, in order to measure changing the world, you have to think in counterfactuals. If you didn't exist, how would the course of history be different?

It shows the form of an office, but with all the real work being done in a datacenter thousands of miles away. Why will the office continue to exist? While it's much easier to imagine a singleton than the ritualistic continuation of offices, that's likely because we're primed by sci-fi. Nature did not collapse to a singleton, and most AI looking things require exponentially more resources to see linear returns. If you've been playing with agent swarms you see this: is it worth 10x the spend to get the work done twice as fast? Is it worth 100x the spend to get the work done three times faster? It breaks down at some point (Amdahl's law).

What I know for sure is that the best we can hope for is fair markets (against monopolies, against internal models, against vertical integration). Linear spend for linear amounts of compute.
