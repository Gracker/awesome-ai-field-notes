## English Abstract

Coding agents succeed or fail in the context humans give them, yet most evaluations measure only output. Drawing on developer experience research, we introduce Agent Experience (AX), an instrument that scores session transcripts on four dimensions of the CAFE(S) framework: Clarity, Actionability, Fidelity, and Efficiency; Security, a gate rather than a friction dimension, is out of scope. A rubric-guided language model rates each dimension from 1 to 5 and explains what the human could have provided. We report on three studies that tested this model. In the Stack Overflow Developer Survey, respondents reporting context problems were less likely to rate AI as very useful in all seven activities examined (12-28%). Across 882 rated agentic sessions from a pilot sample of 1,000, Actionability received the lowest ratings (59.6% of ratings at or below 3, versus 26.6% for Fidelity), and ratings were lower in longer sessions. One researcher's unblinded review of 15 randomly sampled sessions found all scores reasonable and explanations supported. In a controlled scenario, we injected context defects into one data-analysis task and ran it twelve times. Every defect condition used more tokens than the clean baseline (mean +91%), and five evaluator models flagged 50-85% of targeted dimensions and 14-36% of non-targeted dimensions. Fidelity defects were detected most consistently and Actionability defects least. Because interventions were imperfectly isolated, these results are exploratory. AX gives teams structured, actionable evidence about context-related friction that complements output and outcome measures.
