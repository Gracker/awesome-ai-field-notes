## 原文摘录（Evidence Excerpts）

> This is an update to our previous[security bulletin](https://fireworks.ai/security/bulletins/october-2026-security-incident)published on October 9, 2026. In response to the incident, we notified law enforcement and obtained assistance from a leading cybersecurity firm, Palo Alto Networks Unit 42, to help conduct forensic analysis. At the time of publishing the bulletin, only Fireworks stored secrets (e.g. used in evaluation jobs) were known to have been accessed. Since then, we discovered impacts to account and billing information. For a limited set of accounts, we also discovered impact to secrets, datasets, and incomplete models.

> The evidence indicates links to a cybercriminal threat actor group, which is financially motivated and aimed at selling tokens from unauthorized access to Large Language Models (LLMs) to illicit routing gateways, known as "LLMJacking". On November 2, Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant published[a blog post](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/llmjacking-monetizing-compromised-cloud-models)about the rise of LLMJacking, their motives, techniques used, and mitigation.

> A third-party cloud provider that hosted Fireworks R&D machines experienced a security incident on October 23. On October 24, the unauthorized third party used it to reach Fireworks development machines and then run an unauthorized workload that led to additional customer data impact. We detected this activity on the same day, and followed our incident response processes to take immediate remediation measures and close known critical vulnerabilities. As of October 25, we have not detected further evidence of successful unauthorized access by the threat actor on the Fireworks platform customers use.
