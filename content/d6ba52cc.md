## English Summary

Recent controversies — an award-winning work accused of being AI-generated, an author losing a contract after Pangram flagged a manuscript — are really about power, not detection accuracy: who decides who wins literary prizes, who fails students, who gets excluded from public discussion. Goedecke reads much anti-AI sentiment as resistance to society's turn from messy community processes toward tooling that scores you. He still supports detectors because the alternative is worse: people who don't use AI are bad at identifying AI text in both directions, so handing judgment to anti-AI communities yields more errors. Footnotes hit the constraints: people lie, drafts can be generated after the fact, C2PA is not the tool for this. Whether a detector is accurate and whether it should be the judge are separate questions; the latter needs explicit rules and an appeals path.
