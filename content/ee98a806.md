New podcast episode between Ege Erdil and Daniel Kokotajlo. I thought this was going to be a debate, but they ended up agreeing on a lot more than I expected.

Their main point of disagreement is how likely it is that we get some intelligence explosion that gives us AIs more competent than any human expert before 2029. Ege puts this possibility at around 20%, whereas Daniel thinks it’s higher than 50%.

They agree that there is not a huge difference in probability, and that, in either case, the situation is crazy enough that our civilization really needs to grapple with how the world might be transformed, and we need a plan that actually makes sense.

In particular, we should stop antagonizing China if we think some kind of global treaty will be needed to make superintelligence go well.

There was another important crux about how well we should expect future AIs (that swamp humans in population size and cognitive power) to treat us, and how relevant it is that current AIs, by and large, seem to be pretty nice.

We close off with predictions for 2027. Daniel, of course, expects superintelligence next year. Ege’s median for full automation is 2040, but by next year he expects AIs that can beat any video game on Steam, and which can control robots to get simple physical tasks (like folding laundry) done at roughly human speed.
