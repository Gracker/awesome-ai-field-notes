# The Thinking Leakage in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

> 原文链接: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14270

## 要点 (中文)

混合推理模型（如 Qwen3 系列）在 NO-THINK 模式下做 RL 能涨分的流行配方被系统性质疑。作者在 Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B 上用 GRPO 训练，发现增益主要来自 thinking leakage：回复变长变反思、被原模型判为更像自己的思考、内部激活向 THINK 模式偏移；继续放大 RL 更新只会逼近而不超过 THINK 模式精度；把 RL 模型的低维 THINK 相关激活子空间换回原值即抹掉大部分增益，反向移植则把增益转给原模型；GRPO 的 advantage 加权信号天然偏好 THINK 风格回复。结论：NO-THINK 增益很大程度是原模型已有推理能力的再激活。

## Key claims (English)

Training hybrid reasoning models with RL in NO-THINK mode mostly reactivates the original THINK-mode abilities rather than creating new ones: on Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B with GRPO, responses grow more THINK-like, activations shift toward the THINK mode, swapping a low-dimensional THINK-related activation subspace removes most of the RL gain, and the advantage-weighted update itself favors THINK-like responses.

## 原文抓取

arXiv abstract page (submitted 9 Oct 2026)

Authors: Wenxuan Zhu, Shuming Liu, Bang An, Aznaur Aliev, Bing Li, Yibo Yang, Bernard Ghanem
Subjects: 

Hybrid reasoning models such as the Qwen3 series switch between a THINK mode and a NO-THINK mode through a few chat-template tokens. A popular recipe trains such models with reinforcement learning in NO-THINK mode and reports improved reasoning performance. We provide evidence that much of this gain can be explained by thinking leakage: the gain comes largely from partially reactivating the reasoning abilities of the original model&#39;s THINK mode instead of learning enhanced reasoning behavior. We train Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, and 8B with GRPO in NO-THINK mode. As accuracy improves, responses become longer and more reflective, are increasingly judged by the original model to resemble its own thinking, and the model&#39;s internal activations shift toward its THINK mode. We then investigate at the level of weights, activations, and attention heads: scaling the RL update further elicits THINK-mode behavior and approaches THINK-mode accuracy but does not exceed it; in Qwen3-8B, replacing a low-dimensional THINK-related activation subspace of the RL-trained model with its original-model value removes most of the RL gain, and the reverse exchange transfers a considerable fraction of the gain to the original model; and the RL-induced shift passes through the attention heads that read the empty think block. We further identify a possible mechanism in the GRPO training signal: responses favored by the advantage-weighted update are more THINK-like, and a gradient step computed from this signal moves the model toward its THINK state. Thus, gains in NO-THINK mode can largely reflect reactivation of reasoning abilities already present in the original model.
