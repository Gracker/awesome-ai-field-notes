# Bundestrojaner: you can't make users secure by making computers less secure (Cory Doctorow)

> 原文链接: https://pluralistic.net/2026/10/13/petards-r-us/

## 要点 (中文)

Cory Doctorow 10 月 13 日文章：从近期 AI 公司的 hacking 工具失控搞坏别人服务器的事件切入，回拉加密战争史——NSA 曾把可用加密代码列为军火，EFF 与 Cindy Cohen 的 Bernstein 案确立代码受第一修正案保护。核心论点是同义反复式的：不存在只有好人能攻破、坏人攻不破的计算机。NOBUS 学说囤积的漏洞以每年约 20% 的速率被独立复用，Eternal Blue 泄漏后变成 WannaCry 勒索软件打穿医院银行；德国海关的 Bundestrojaner「合法拦截」软件本身就漏洞百出，任何人都能接管被感染机器。文章批评 chat control、age verification 与加密禁令仍在重复同一个错误。

## Key claims (English)

Doctorow ties recent AI-company hacking-tool incidents to the crypto wars: NOBUS-hoarded vulnerabilities get independently rediscovered at ~20% per year (Eternal Blue -> WannaCry), Germany's Bundestrojaner lawful-interware was itself insecure, and chat control / age verification proposals repeat the same error — you cannot make users secure by making their computers less secure.

# Bundestrojaner ([permalink](https://pluralistic.net/2026/10/13/petards-r-us/#nobus))

Take cybersecurity, subject that is filling today's headlines, primarily due to a spate of poorly understood (and criminally poorly explained) incidents in which AI companies' hacking tools broke into various servers on the internet, sometimes trashing them [...]

This is an old, old, _old_ fight. It was arguably the first policy fight of the digital era: the fight over whether we could have working encryption _at all_. Old hands call this era "the crypto wars" (from when "crypto" meant "cryptography"), when the NSA classed working encryption code as a "munition" and criminalized civilian access to it. [...]

"You cannot make a computer user more secure by making their computers less secure." We don't know how to make a computer that only "good guys" can break into, but which "bad guys" bounce off of. Either we make computers more secure, or we don't.

The worst culprits here are governments [...] the state security apparatuses of the world's governments keep insisting that _all_ working computer security must be dismantled, so they can spy on all of us in order to figure out whom among us is a Bad Guy [...]

A series of leaks of computer vulnerabilities hoarded by both the NSA and CIA under NOBUS confirmed that these bugs were independently rediscovered by criminals and state actors at the rate of about 20% per year. The highest-profile leak was an NSA-hoarded vulnerability in Windows they codenamed "Eternal Blue." Eternal Blue leaked in 2017, whereupon cybercriminals hybridized it with some otherwise undistinguished ransomware to create Wannacry [...]

Perhaps the most egregious example of that are the Bundestrojaners, malicious software purchased by the German government, whose customs agents secretly installed it on the computers of unsuspecting people during border inspections. This "lawful interception" software was hugely invasive: it could grab your screen, plunder your hard drive, and install and execute software on your computer [...] But it wasn't merely invasive - it was insecure, so anyone (rival spies, criminals, stalkers) could scan the internet for infected computers and take them over.

Despite this, governments continue to demand the introduction of grave, universal secure vulnerabilities into our digital lives. Take "chat control," a proposal to force every device manufacturer to install software that continuously scans your devices for child sex abuse material [...]
