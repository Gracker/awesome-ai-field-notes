# [Jensen Huang Was Always Going to Lose](https://sharptext.net/2026/jensen-huang-was-always-going-to-lose/)

Thursday, November 12, 2026

![](https://i0.wp.com/sharptext.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-2286326226.jpg?resize=1024%2C683&quality=89&ssl=1)

Tomohiro Ohsumi/Getty Images

There are decades where nothing happens, and there are weeks when decades happen. The CEO and founder of Nvidia just experienced the latter, as did many in tech, media, and government. I have a variety of thoughts after spending the past several days reading and recording podcasts about these (figurative and literal) affairs, but let me express one unpopular sentiment at the outset: what happened to Jensen Huang this week is tragic and depressing.

I have a disease that makes me sympathetic to almost anyone who ends up on the wrong end of viral outrage and panic, and unfortunately it’s flaring up again. Huang is one of the greatest CEOs in American history. He’s still hyper competent, he’s been incredibly important to the growth of the AI ecosystem, and frankly, he’s among the most sane of the contemporary tech leaders we have on offer. His fall from grace has been not only stunning but seemingly coordinated. It’s been painful to watch on a human level, and of course it has ominous implications for the tech industry. Regardless, though: what’s done is done. Huang’s reputation has been irreparably harmed, his wife and family have been humiliated over the past two weeks, and he just lost control of the world-conquering company that he founded 33 years ago, immediately throwing Nvidia’s future into a world of post-Jobsian uncertainty.

It’s an incredible coup for the Ministry of State Security if this was all a Chinese op, but honestly, I’m not convinced it was. That’s point number two. The entire media establishment and most of our government seem to have taken it as a given that Alice Huang was a spy for the MSS, but a) the sourcing there is based entirely on the vague and classified claims of intelligence services who despise Huang, while b) “spy for the MSS” can mean different things. Was she a 43-year-old operative trained by the MSS to honeypot powerful executives and obtain proprietary information, or was she a businesswoman who was (like all other Chinese citizens) obligated to cooperate with the MSS when the agency discovered her affair with Jensen? I would bet on some version of the latter (“asset,” not spy), and I think it’s more likely the spy claims were invented altogether ([were they?](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/12/politics/alice-huang-us-intel-jensen-huang-nvidia)) than it is that Alice Huang was some kind of Chinese Keri Russell executing a classic sexual blackmail scheme.

All that noted, I truly don’t know. We may never know. And one thing is certain: if Alice Huang was spending billions of Chinese investor dollars to obtain high-end Nvidia hardware that was banned for export to Chinese companies and may have later been smuggled into mainland China … she was not doing that without approval from the Chinese government. So, not a CCP agent, but not _not_ a CCP agent_—_and definitely not someone that Huang should have felt comfortable sleeping with. How in the world could he be so stupid?

On the other hand, should we really be surprised? Whenever Jensen Huang discusses China, I’m reminded of Upton Sinclair: “It is difficult to get a man to understand something, when his salary depends on his not understanding it.”

Here was Huang [in a recent interview with Bloomberg](https://x.com/business/status/2119605028826513673?a=xMqVLaUKIiFPxVP0734&s=20), answering Emily Chang’s question about engagement with China’s tech sector by saying, “We must work with them in every possible way.” Then there was [Huang talking to Dwarkesh Patel in April](https://x.com/dwarkesh_sp/status/2044456498441708013?a=ucTsGgjmmMdQ9ATMG24&s=20), going viral when he angrily called it “lunacy” to worry about the CCP and PLA using advanced American hardware to execute AI cyberattacks. Equally infamous around D.C. was [Huang’s conversation with Brad Gerstner last year](https://x.com/lukedepulford/status/2119703464880341487), in which he declared that the label “China hawk” is a “badge of shame.”

Both Huang and his company were lobbying against stepped-up export controls on AI chips as early as 2022, and were (legally) circumventing the spirit and expressed purpose of those export controls [with the A800 chip in 2022](https://www.reuters.com/technology/exclusive-nvidia-offers-new-advanced-chip-china-that-meets-us-export-controls-2022-11-08/), [the H800 in 2023](https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidia-tweaks-flagship-h100-chip-export-china-h800-2023-03-21/), and [the H20 in 2024](https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidia-launch-china-focused-ai-chip-q2-2024-sources-2024-01-08/). He’s continued to lobby against export controls throughout the Trump administration and continued his attempts to sell AI chips to both mainland China customers and China-linked entities in Malaysia, Thailand and Singapore. To be fair, of course, anyone who’s followed this space for the past several years will remember that Nvidia has been [joined by Intel, Qualcomm and everyone else in the American semiconductor industry](https://www.reuters.com/technology/us-chip-lobby-presses-biden-refrain-further-china-curbs-2023-07-17/) in lobbying multiple administrations against new China restrictions. Likewise, [Google, Microsoft, and Oracle all have sizable footprints in Southeast Asia](https://www.reuters.com/technology/google-microsoft-offer-nvidia-chips-chinese-companies-information-reports-2024-07-17/) providing Chinese customers with access to high-end compute.

Huang has been by far the loudest and most powerful of those voices, though, and now he will fall the hardest. That’s one way to understand what happened over the past two weeks: Jensen had a giant target on his back among many in tech and government who favored stricter restrictions on Chinese chip access, his mistakes with Alice Huang and Megaspeed gave those critics ammunition, and for reasons that remain unclear (but fun to wonder about!), some of those folks have decided to pull the trigger now.
