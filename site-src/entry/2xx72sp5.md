## English

THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS

"THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS" refers to a speculative report published in February 2026 by Citrini Research, authored by James van Geelen and Alap Shah. This widely discussed "thought exercise" outlines a scenario where the rapid advancement and widespread adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) lead to a significant global economic downturn by June 2028.

The core premise of the report, also termed the "Intelligence Displacement Spiral," posits that increasingly capable AI agents displace white-collar workers at an accelerated rate. This displacement leads to a collapse in consumer spending due to widespread unemployment and reduced incomes, which subsequently triggers cascading failures across various sectors, including the mortgage market and private credit.

The report suggests that while AI drives unprecedented productivity and GDP growth, this "Ghost GDP" does not translate into consumer demand because machines do not consume, thus breaking the traditional economic cycle.

## Key Predictions

Citrini Research's scenario painted a gloomy picture, envisioning:
* 10.2% unemployment rate
* 38% cumulative drawdown in the S&P 500 from its October 2026 highs by June 2028
* Collapse in consumer spending due to widespread unemployment
* Cascading failures across mortgage market and private credit sectors

## Reception and Debate

The report gained considerable attention upon its release, reportedly causing stock market fluctuations in major tech and financial firms and sparking debates among economists and industry experts.

While some lauded its comprehensive modeling of potential "left tail risks" associated with AI, others criticized its economic logic and offered rebuttals, arguing that it missed the adaptive responses of humans and the potential for reinvestment of AI-driven savings into growth.
