# THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS

## Report Overview

"THE 2028 GLOBAL INTELLIGENCE CRISIS" is a highly discussed report by Citrini Research, authored by James van Geelen and Alap Shah, and published in February 2026. This macro-analysis is presented as a "thought exercise in financial history, from the future," specifically a macro memo from June 2028, detailing the progression and fallout of a "Global Intelligence Crisis."

## The Ghost GDP Concept

The report explores the potential impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on the global economy, depicting a future where AI drives significant increases in productivity and corporate profits, leading to what the authors term "Ghost GDP." This "Ghost GDP" refers to economic output that appears in national accounts but does not circulate through the real economy due to widespread white-collar job displacement and suppressed wages for human workers.

## Economic Projections

In the scenario outlined by Citrini Research, the human-centric consumer economy, which constituted 70% of GDP at the time, withers as companies require fewer workers due to advancing AI capabilities. This leads to a "human intelligence displacement spiral," where white-collar workers experience a structural impairment of their earnings power and spending.

The report projects a grim economic outlook, including:
- National unemployment rate reaching 10.2%
- Cumulative drawdown in the S&P 500 of 38% from its October 2026 highs by June 2028

## Market Impact

The report gained significant traction online, reportedly garnering millions of views and being amplified by notable investors like Michael Burry. Its release was associated with a fall in the stock market prices of major technology and financial firms, with some sources even attributing market movements to the article.

## Critical Reception

Despite its impact, the authors clarified that the report was intended as a scenario analysis and a speculative "thought experiment," not a direct prediction. It has also faced criticism for its economic logic, with some economists and analysts arguing that it may confuse the distribution of wealth with its outright destruction.

Critics suggest that while AI may reallocate labor savings, these savings don't vanish but rather reallocate through various economic channels such as lower prices, capital expenditure, profits, dividends, and tax revenue.
